1. A next-generation BAS reporting system should primarily generate organization-wide attack resilience reports that aggregate results across all endpoints, identities, cloud assets, and attack paths rather than focusing only on individual machines. The report should begin with an executive-level overview showing the organization’s overall attack resilience score, most critical exposure paths, highest-risk assets, prevention effectiveness, detection latency, lateral movement risk, and the likelihood of attackers reaching crown-jewel systems.

2. The report should visually map how simulated adversaries progressed through the environment, where security controls failed or succeeded, what objectives were achieved before detection, and how far an attacker could realistically move across the enterprise. It should provide attack-path visibility that helps organizations understand attacker progression depth, privilege escalation opportunities, and potential blast radius across interconnected systems.

3. From there, the report should progressively drill down into campaign-level results, endpoint-specific findings, attack timelines, evidence collected, telemetry generated, detections triggered, process trees, privilege escalation paths, cleanup validation, and confidence/reliability scores for every simulation. Each executed simulation should clearly display structured classifications such as BLOCKED, DETECTED, ALLOWED, DETECTED_AFTER_OBJECTIVE, PRECONDITION_FAILED, or INTERRUPTED along with supporting evidence and execution context.

4. Instead of simply listing ATT&CK techniques or pass/fail results, the report should prioritize real business risk, attack progression depth, blast radius potential, and actionable remediation guidance. The final reporting experience should provide meaningful operational intelligence for executives, SOC teams, security engineers, and purple teams simultaneously, enabling organizations to understand not just which attacks were tested, but how realistically attackers could compromise critical assets within the environment.
